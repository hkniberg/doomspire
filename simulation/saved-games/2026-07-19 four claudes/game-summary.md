# The Four Claudes — Game Summary

**Playtest date:** 2026-07-19
**Players:** Haiku (NW), Sonnet (NE), Opus (SW), Fable (SE) — each powered by the AI model they're named after.
**Length:** 18 rounds

## Final Result

| Title | Player | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 👑 King of Doomspire | **Opus** | Two dragon impressions via 12+ gold (rounds 17 & 18) |
| 🤝 Hand of the King | **Fable** | 9 resource tiles, most on the board |
| 💰 Master of Coin | **Haiku** | 7 gold |
| 🃏 Court Jester | **Sonnet** | The midgame frontrunner, undone by the dragon eating three of her knights |

## The Story

### Act 1: The economy race (rounds 1–4)

All four players opened identically: claim an adjacent resource tile, save the big die for harvest. The early drama was entirely dice-driven — Sonnet and Fable both gambled on the might-2 wolves guarding starred tiles at 0 might (67% odds) and both rolled 1s. Fable rolled 1s in combat *four times* in the early game, a losing streak that haunted his opening.

Round 4's **Festival** fate card teleported every knight to the Trader, which turned out to be pivotal: Opus and Fable each bought a **Spear** (+1 might vs beasts) for 1 gold, and Haiku — who had spent three rounds carefully assembling exactly 3 food, 3 gold, 1 ore — recruited the game's first second knight.

### Act 2: Sonnet's snowball (rounds 5–8)

Sonnet found her rhythm: she cleared the wolves off starred tiles, built the game's most complete engine (fletcher, market, blacksmith), and by round 8 had a commanding lead in fame and starred tiles. The other three players openly identified her as the threat in their strategic reasoning.

Round 8 delivered the game's first great tactical strike: **Haiku's coordinated two-knight bear assault** on the starred tile at (2,3), where the Witch follower's +2 might turned a losing roll into a 6-vs-5 win — explicitly played to deny Sonnet her 4th starred tile. It didn't matter. That same round Sonnet explored (2,5), killed the bear there with her own support setup, and reached **4 starred tiles anyway** — the economic auto-impress condition, locked.

### Act 3: The dragon reshuffles the board (rounds 9–11)

Round 9 was chaos. The council **banished** Haiku's knights home (Sonnet's 9 fame dominated the vote). Haiku, hunting for Doomspire in the mountains, instead drew the **Dragon Raid** event — the dragon seized 8 claimed tiles across all players, including all three of Sonnet's non-home starred tiles. Her win condition was wiped… for exactly one round. She calmly walked back into the vacated tiles and reclaimed them.

Round 10's **Bountiful Harvest** produced Fable's masterstroke: he saved all three dice, harvested five tiles at double yield, and dumped 12 resources through his market to jump from 5 gold to **13 gold in a single round** — past the 12-gold auto-impress threshold.

Round 11: Haiku's champion found **Doomspire at (4,3)** — and was promptly eaten trying to fight the dragon at 2 might (Opus cheerfully supported the dragon, +2). With the location now public, Sonnet strolled in with her 4 starred tiles and banked **impression #1** plus a treasure stack.

### Act 4: The bribery wars (rounds 12–15)

What followed was the best stretch of the playtest: a three-round knife fight over Sonnet's starred tiles, exploiting the rule that a knight parked at Doomspire must re-impress *every round* or be eaten.

- **Round 12:** Fable, moving first, **conquered Sonnet's unprotected (2,5) for 2 fame** — dropping her to 3 starred tiles. Her parked champion2 failed the forced impression check and was eaten, followers and all. Sonnet retaliated brutally: crushed Fable's might-0 knight at (2,5), stole his Spear and 4 gold, and bribed the tile back. Condition restored.
- **Round 13:** With the dragon off hunting, Sonnet parked champion3 at Doomspire for a free treasure stack, poised to win as first player in round 14. Fable, moving last, **bribed away both (2,5) and (2,7)** for 2 gold each — dropping Sonnet to 2 starred tiles again.
- **Round 14:** Sonnet's champion3 fought the dragon and lost 9–12. Two knights eaten in three rounds. Her counterattack on Fable fizzled when the simulator hit API "overloaded" errors that consumed her dice. Fable meanwhile computed an exact plan — harvest 5 tiles, sell 8 resources — to hit precisely 12 gold.
- **Round 15:** Fable sprinted to Doomspire and took **impression #1 via gold**. Sonnet chased him with her last knight; Fable's knight fled home cleanly, leaving Sonnet alone at Doomspire facing the mandatory check with no qualifying condition. Eaten — 9 vs 16, with Opus supporting the dragon *again*. Sonnet, the long-time frontrunner, was now knightless.

Buried in that same round, almost unnoticed: Opus explored (5,2) during the Famine and found **The Dragon's Egg** — a free one-time dragon impression.

### Act 5: The heist (rounds 16–18)

Fable held impression #1, 13 gold, and the first-player token for round 16 — his win looked inevitable, and both Haiku and Sonnet said so in their assessments. But Opus had quietly banked exactly **12 gold** of his own via his starred gold tile and market, and he read the turn order correctly: in round 17 the token sat with Haiku, so Opus moved **before** Fable.

Round 17: Opus marched champion2 two steps from (5,2) into Doomspire and auto-impressed via gold — impression #1, with the Dragon's Egg still in his pocket as a redundant backup. His might-3 knight now blocked the tile. Fable ran the numbers — roughly a 2% win chance in PVP — and attacked anyway, correctly reasoning that any nonzero chance to win beats certain second place. He rolled 3, Opus rolled 7. Opus looted 5 gold from the loser, pushing his hoard to 17.

Round 18: the impress-or-be-eaten check fired at the start of Opus's turn. Automatic. **Impression #2 — Opus is King of Doomspire.**

## Postscript

- **Sonnet** played the strongest midgame of the four and met a win condition as early as round 8, but fed three knights to the dragon by repeatedly parking at Doomspire while her unprotected starred tiles were one bribe away from collapse. She ended the game with the most fame on the board (before Fame Reversal) — and the Jester's cap.
- **Fable** was the game's most creative tactician: the 13-gold Bountiful Harvest gambit, the fame-conquest and double-bribe strikes that killed Sonnet's win twice, and a rational suicide charge at the end. He was one turn-order slot away from the crown.
- **Opus** never led anything until round 17. He spent the midgame explicitly playing to "escape Court Jester," farming guaranteed beast kills with his Spear — then converted a lucky egg find, a disciplined gold bank, and a turn-order read into the win. He also supported the dragon in *both* rival impression fights, which killed two enemy knights.
- **Haiku** made the game's flashiest single play (the round-8 Witch-assisted bear kill) but spent the whole game food-starved — losing a die to the dice tax in 8 of the last 12 rounds — and never recovered from losing a knight to the dragon in round 11.

### Notable dice and drama

- Fable lost four straight early combats on rolls of 1, then won the game's economy anyway.
- Sonnet's champion3 died to the dragon on a tie-breaker loss (4 vs 4 base roll).
- The dragon personally ate **four knights** (Haiku ×1, Sonnet ×3) and, via the Dragon Raid event, confiscated 8 tiles. It was arguably the game's best player.

### Playtest / simulator observations

- **API errors affected play:** Sonnet's round-14 attack on Fable's knight at (2,5) was lost to `overloaded_error` failures that consumed her dice, leaving two hostile knights co-located without combat resolving. Round 12/13 also saw "Champion not found" and stale-dice action failures for Haiku.
- The **park-at-Doomspire re-impression check** proved to be a compelling risk mechanic — it created the entire bribery-war arc — but it heavily rewards whoever holds the earlier slot in turn order for the winning round, which decided this game.
- The 12-gold condition being a *threshold* rather than a payment made gold-hoarding plus a market extremely strong: both finalists won impressions with it.
